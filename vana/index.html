PART IV · ĀRAṆYAKA & VIRĀṬA PARVASTWELVE YEARS OF WILDERNESS & ONE YEAR OF SHADOWS

Exiles in the ashes,
phantoms in the court.

Twelve years forging celestial weapons in the wild;
one year hiding in plain sight.

The last throw was absolute: twelve years in the forest, and a thirteenth lived unrecognised. If they were found before its final sunset, the twelve years would begin again. Śakuni threw. The Pāṇḍavas put on bark and deerskin, and Kuntī stayed behind in Vidura's house.

Vidura described their going to the blind king. Yudhiṣṭhira walked with his face covered, so his gaze would not burn the city. Bhīma walked flexing his arms; Arjuna scattered sand, as he would one day scatter arrows; Sahadeva smeared his face; Nakula covered himself in dust. Draupadī walked with her loose hair over her face, and said that one day the wives of those who mocked her would weep the same way.

INDRAKĪLA · THE HIMALAYAARJUNA AND THE HUNTER

He fought a hunter
for a single boar,
and found he was
fighting a god.

Arjuna went alone into the mountains to win the weapons of the gods, and held a penance so fierce the forest grew hot. A boar charged him; he and a wild hunter of the hills shot it in the same instant. They argued, then fought: arrows, then the bow itself, then bare hands, until Arjuna fell senseless. The hunter was Śiva, and in his delight he gave Arjuna the Pāśupata weapon.

Arjuna lived five years in Indra's heaven learning the arms of the gods. When the nymph Urvaśī came to him at night he honoured her as an elder of his line and refused her; she cursed him to live a year as one who is not a man. Indra made the curse a gift: it would hide him in the thirteenth year. Meanwhile in the forest Duryodhana, come to gloat, was taken captive by the Gandharvas, and was freed at Yudhiṣṭhira's command by the very cousins he had come to mock.

GANDHAMĀDANATHE OLD MONKEY ON THE PATH

The strongest man alive
could not lift
an old monkey's tail.

A wind carried a golden, thousand-petalled lotus to Draupadī's feet, and she asked Bhīma for more. He climbed the Gandhamādana mountain, roaring and shaking the trees, until an old monkey lying across the path asked him, gently, to step around. Bhīma told him to move his tail. The monkey said he was too old: lift it yourself.

Bhīma could not move it at all. The monkey was Hanumān, son of the Wind like Bhīma himself: his elder brother. He showed Bhīma the form in which he had leapt the ocean for Rāma, and promised that in the war he would sit on Arjuna's banner and roar with Bhīma's voice.

THE FOREST · THE TWELFTH YEARTHE LAKE OF THE YAKṢA

“What is the greatest wonder?”
Every day the living
watch the dead go,
and believe they will not.

Searching for water, the brothers came one by one to a still lake. A voice forbade them to drink before answering its questions. Nakula, Sahadeva, Arjuna and Bhīma drank anyway, and fell dead on the bank.

Yudhiṣṭhira stood before the voice and answered. Satisfied, the Yakṣa offered him one brother's life. He chose Nakula: his father's other queen, Mādrī, should also have a living son. The Yakṣa revealed himself as Dharma, Yudhiṣṭhira's own father, raised all four, and promised that no one would know them in the year to come.

THE COURT OF VIRĀṬATHE YEAR IN DISGUISE

The greatest royals on earth
became servants.

They hid their weapons in a śamī tree outside the city of Virāṭa, wrapped like a corpse so no one would touch them, and walked in as strangers. Yudhiṣṭhira became Kaṅka, a brahmin who played dice with the king. Bhīma became Ballava, the cook. Arjuna, wearing Urvaśī's curse, became Bṛhannalā, with braid and bangles, teaching dance to the princess Uttarā. Nakula kept the horses as Granthika, Sahadeva the cattle as Tantipāla.

Draupadī, empress of the earth, became Sairandhrī, who dressed the queen's hair.

THE COURT OF VIRĀṬATHE DANCE HALL AT NIGHT

He came to the dance hall
for a woman.
Bhīma was waiting.

The queen's brother Kīcaka, commander of the army, desired Sairandhrī. When she refused him, he kicked her to the ground in the open court, and the king said nothing. That night she went to Bhīma in the kitchens and wept.

She told Kīcaka to come to the dark dance hall alone. Bhīma lay there under a woman's veil. By morning Kīcaka was dead, his body broken past recognition, and the city whispered that Sairandhrī's Gandharva husbands had come for him.

OUTSIDE VIRĀṬATHE WEAPONS IN THE TREE

A dance teacher
asked for a chariot.

Kīcaka's death told Duryodhana that Bhīma was near. The whole Kuru army marched on Virāṭa's cattle to force the Pāṇḍavas into the open, and with the king's forces away, only the boy prince Uttara was left to ride out, with Bṛhannalā as his charioteer.

At the sight of the Kuru host the prince tried to run. Bṛhannalā drove him to the śamī tree, had him climb for the bundle, and took down the Gāṇḍīva. Then he told the boy his ten names. The thirteenth year had ended; Arjuna blew his conch Devadatta.

THE CATTLE RAIDARJUNA ALONE

One archer.
An army asleep.

Arjuna drove back Karṇa, Droṇa, Kṛpa and Bhīṣma one after another, then loosed the Sammohana weapon, and the whole Kuru host slept where it stood. He took no lives. He sent Uttara among the sleeping kings to bring back their bright upper cloths for the princess's dolls.

The Pāṇḍavas revealed themselves to King Virāṭa, and his daughter Uttarā was married to Arjuna's son, Abhimanyu. The exile was over. The war was not.

THE STORY DIVIDES

A The Yakṣa's questions Answer what Dharma asked at the lake. Open each question. Open this path

Seven of the questions,
and Yudhiṣṭhira's answers.

What is heavier than the earth?

A mother.

What is higher than the sky?

A father.

What is swifter than the wind?

The mind.

What is more numerous than the grass?

Our worries.

Who is truly happy?

One who owes no debt and need not wander from home, though he cooks only a few greens at evening.

What is the greatest wonder?

Day after day the living watch creatures go to the house of death, yet those who remain believe they will live for ever.

What is the path?

Argument has no end, scriptures differ, and no sage is the only authority. The truth of dharma is hidden in a cave; the path is the one the great have walked.

Retold from the Āraṇyaka Parva in our own words; the Yakṣa asks many more.

THE RIVERBANK · DAWNKARṆA'S ARMOUR

No one who asked Karṇa
was ever refused.
Not even a god.

Karṇa was born with armour grown into his skin and earrings of light: while he wore them, no weapon could kill him. Indra, Arjuna's father, resolved to take them. The Sun warned his son in a dream: a brahmin will come at your prayer and ask for your armour. Refuse him.

Karṇa answered that he had never refused anyone who asked at his prayer, and would not begin with a god. When the brahmin came, Karṇa cut the armour and earrings from his own body and gave them. Shamed, Indra gave him in return the Śakti, a spear that would kill any one enemy, once. Karṇa saved it for Arjuna.
