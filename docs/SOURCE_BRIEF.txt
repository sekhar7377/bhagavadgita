What the studio itself confirms
The studio describes an atmospheric, minimal design using bas-relief and sculpted Roman numerals, with a separate backstage experience. These published explanations support the broad architecture. The additional public-code inspection establishes the specific relief mechanism below. The studio’s full deployment configuration, original sculpting process and complete animation system remain outside this audit.
The central technical finding
The opening uses actual relief meshes. A small GPU field retains successive pointer stamps, with animated noise and a second programmed path adding variation. The vertex shader uses that field to change the sculpture’s depth; the fragment shader simultaneously blends packed shading states so the apparent shadows follow the reveal. A plaster texture joins the sculptures and background into one mineral surface. Scrolling shifts the field and recycles sections. [R8–R9]

This is why a finished illustration alone cannot deliver the requested effect. The production needs original Gītā relief geometry, matching shading data and a coordinated interaction system. Section J specifies that work in detail. The automatic path is initialized by the relief scene; it is not established as an idle-only feature. A stationary pointer can also continue contributing coverage, so “pointer stopped” and “all field injection stopped” are different tests.

Core experience: the visitor approaches what appears to be a quiet stone surface. Movement reveals a carved world. Scrolling moves through Arjuna’s crisis, Krishna’s teaching, the vision of the universal form, and Arjuna’s recovered clarity. Every interaction should support attention, discovery, contemplation or understanding.

E. Content authority and narrative boundaries
Use the Bhagavad Gītā’s 18 chapters as the source structure. Verify Sanskrit, chapter names, verse references and speaker changes against a reliable textual source such as IIT Kanpur’s Gita Supersite/Heritage resources and a clearly identified edition.

Choose and document a numbering convention. A commonly used recension has 700 verses; some editions include an additional question at the start of Chapter 13 and count 701. Do not combine numbering schemes silently. The reader’s count, citations, search and chapter totals must agree.

The dialogue occurs within the Mahābhārata’s battlefield setting and its narrative frame. Do not turn every teaching metaphor into an event that literally occurs in the story. Mark symbolic artworks as interpretations. Do not present Krishna and Arjuna as traveling physically to mountains, cosmic observatories or other locations during every chapter.

Keep these content layers distinct:
- Sanskrit source text: verified, exact and rendered as text.
- Translation: attributed and legally reusable.
- Editorial paraphrase: original contemporary explanation, identified as such.
- Interpretive note: contextual explanation that can acknowledge differing philosophical readings.
- Visual metaphor: artistic interpretation, not evidence of a literal scene.
Never fabricate a verse or attribute an invented quotation to Krishna. Do not reduce karma-yoga to indifference about consequences. Do not frame dharma as a universal instruction to obey every authority. Do not erase the ethical tension of Arjuna’s situation. When discussing caste, gender or contested interpretation, use precise contextual language rather than endorsing social hierarchy.

Write clear English. Include exact Sanskrit chapter titles and optional transliteration. An English experience with a verified Sanskrit reader is the initial scope; do not display nonfunctional language switches. Additional languages are complete only when their content and type rendering have been checked.

G. Screen-by-screen direction
G1. Loading and entry
Start with a lightweight mineral surface, a small identity and genuine loading progress for critical assets. Do not invent a percentage that reaches 100 while the experience remains blocked. The page must remain useful if WebGL or a large asset fails. Load fonts and a small poster first; decode the first relief resources before switching to interactive rendering.

The desktop opening should retain the reference’s horizontal balance: identity around the left tenth, main line near the central band, scroll cue to the right. Keep the content’s visual center near half the viewport height. This is a starting composition, not a rigid pixel map.

No giant rectangular primary button. Provide a small semantic “Begin” action for users who need it. Use a quiet 2–3 px cursor mark only on fine-pointer devices, with a visible native-cursor fallback. Sound begins muted; show an intelligible sound control.
G2. The carved opening world
Compose a shallow Kurukṣetra relief with the chariot wheel at the lower left, horses moving through the right side, a banner near an outer edge and Krishna with Arjuna in the midground. Keep faces and the primary narrative objects out of the central text-safe strip. The carving should emerge from the same stone as the wall.

At rest, the mineral surface is legible and calm. Pointer movement raises local sculpture depth and resolves its shading through overlapping organic trails. A separate quiet automatic path intermittently reveals details elsewhere, as the reference does. A stationary pointer and a pointer that has left the surface must behave deliberately; test coverage and velocity separately. Preserve text contrast independently. This screen cannot pass its fidelity gate with a beauty image and a circular opacity mask.
G3. Premise, film and pause
Scroll brings in the opening editorial statement, left aligned with intentional line breaks and generous space. Follow with one broad atmospheric media window. If a true video has not been produced, use a real-time layered scene or an explicitly labeled animated still; do not claim it is generated film.

Then offer a centered paragraph of two to four lines explaining the setting and the reader’s journey. Keep long explanations out of this section. Introduce the reading alternative unobtrusively.
G4. Chapter sequence
Use alternating wide and narrow media with offset text blocks. A chapter can have a broad artwork on the left, a concise passage on the right, then a smaller close detail farther down on the opposite side. Do not repeat identical left-image/right-copy sections eighteen times.

Use blank space as pacing. Alternate visual scale, density, lightness and narrative intensity. The page’s pale relief may continue quietly behind the editorial sequence. A chapter’s localized motifs must be geographically coherent with the artwork.

Feature Chapter 11 as a genuine dramatic peak. Approach it with stillness, expand the visual scale, transition to a dark field, then restore human scale in Chapter 12. Constant climax destroys the story’s rhythm.
G5. Chapter index
Use a typographic index resembling the reference’s project archive. On desktop, keep a stable preview region at left, a narrow column for the chapter number/theme, and titles in the right column. Hover/focus changes the preview and draws a hairline underline. The entire row must be a real link. Inactive labels may soften but remain readable.

Provide optional theme filters such as Action, Knowledge, Meditation and Devotion. These are editorial cross-references, not claims that each chapter belongs exclusively to one category. Expose the selected filter, result count and “All chapters” reset. No hidden-only filtering.
G6. Dark menu
Opening the menu transforms the pale surface into a dark carved world. Use the same underlying compositional family with different material and illumination. A narrow organic opening may disclose a restrained warm interior light; avoid a giant bloom that washes out text.

Menu items have short labels at rest. Hover or keyboard focus can reveal a longer description, a numeral or preview, and a small directional cue. Keep layout motion restrained so links do not flee the pointer. Dark textural depth must remain visible behind the menu.

Escape closes the menu, focus returns to its trigger, body scrolling is correctly restored, and the underlying narrative position is preserved. Trap focus only while the modal is actually open. Remove inactive content from the accessibility tree appropriately.

J3. Build the coordinated rendering pipeline
Implement this sequence under one animation clock:
1. Normalize input. Track pointer position, velocity, entry/leave and input type. Use the canvas’s actual content rectangle and the renderer’s pixel ratio consistently. Keep semantic link hit testing on the true pointer, independent of any decorative cursor lag.
2. Update the field. Sample the previous field with the scroll reprojection offset, attenuate it, add separate user and automatic stamps, constrain channel ranges, and swap the render targets. Never read from the same texture currently being written.
3. Project into the sculpture. Determine screen coordinates from the undeformed vertex/camera state, sample the field, and derive the local relief factor. The source uses screen-space field lookup, not a prepainted per-object hole.
4. Change real depth. Compress each sculpted vertex toward its wall plane and restore it according to that factor. Use a consistent local axis and wall origin. The reference scales local Z; a differently oriented asset needs an equivalent coordinate transform, not an arbitrary world-Z multiplication.
5. Resolve the surface shading. Blend the correctly aligned baked stages as the relief resolves, then apply the mineral texture, restrained broad gradient and any justified secondary response. Geometry and shading must stay visually coupled during reveal and recession.
6. Composite the experience. Render the continuous wall and relief behind the readable DOM interface, with appropriate depth/occlusion for media layers. Use a smooth radial stamp with aspect correction. In the reference, horizontal distance is multiplied by viewport aspect before evaluating falloff. For the source decay rule, define d = 0.953 and step = min(frameDeltaMs, 32) / 16. The previous field’s multiplier is 1 / (1 + step × (1/d − 1)). Reproject the previous field, multiply it by that factor, then add the noise-modulated stamps weighted by step. The source upper-clamps the field at 1 and lower-clamps RGB at −1. Preserve signed velocity and bounded nonnegative coverage appropriately in the new implementation.

Do not substitute the older generic maximum-of-brush-and-mask model for this accumulated field.
