# Visual reference sheet: the Mahābhārata as Vyāsa describes it

Compiled by Gemini (web search over the K. M. Ganguli translation) on 16 November 2026, at the user's request, as binding rules for every carving.
Items marked [LATER TRADITION] are popular iconography not found in Vyāsa's text.
Verify against the text before citing any section number on the site.

This reference sheet summarizes the visual descriptions from K. M. Ganguli’s translation of Vyasa’s Mahabharata (primarily Bhishma, Drona, Karna, and Shalya Parvas, with foundational descriptions from Adi, Vana, Virata, and Udyoga Parvas). Items marked [LATER TRADITION] indicate popular iconography that is absent from or contradicted by Vyasa’s Sanskrit text. These rules will strictly govern all high-relief carving designs in this conversation.   
1. War Chariots (Ratha)

Size & Proportions: Described as monumental, "resembling a city" or "like unto a cloud" in height and bulk (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas), built of heavy timber plated with gold, silver, and iron (Karna Parva).

Protective Walls (Varutha / Fences): Surrounded by high, curved wooden side-fences (kashtha-varutha) plated with metal, studded with gems, or draped in tiger and leopard skins (Udyoga, Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas).

Wheels (Chakra): Standard war chariots have two large wheels (rare celestial cars like Krishna’s or Indra’s have four) with heavy naves, spokes, and golden/iron felloes (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). Two warriors (Chakrarakshas — e.g., Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas for Arjuna) ride/fight on the flanks to guard the wheels (Drona, Karna Parvas).   
Flagstaff & Banners (Dhvaja & Ketana): A towering wooden or golden mast rises from the rear/center of the chariot box, topped by a three-dimensional sculpted metal/jeweled emblem (dhvaja) with a long cloth streamer (pataka / ketana) fluttering below it (Bhishma, Drona Parvas).

Parasols (Chhatra): Royal commanders (Yudhishthira, Bhishma, Duryodhana, Shalya) fight beneath a large white, gem-fringed parasol mounted behind/above them (Bhishma, Drona, Shalya Parvas).

Bells & Ornaments: Chariot frames, yokes, and banner-staffs are hung with rows of small tinkling golden bells (kinkini), floral garlands, and networks of pearls/gems (Drona, Karna Parvas).

Weapons Carried: Each chariot acts as a mobile armory containing dozens of spare bows, multiple large quivers (upasanga / tunira) lashed to the sides/back, lances, darts, maces, swords, shields, and conch shells (Drona, Karna Parvas).   
Horses & Yoking: Drawn by four steeds yoked abreast to a single central pole (isha) and wooden yoke (yuga) via leather traces and girth-bands (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas) — two inner pole-horses (dhurya) and two outer trace-horses (parshni). Steeds wear golden breastplates, plumes/crests, and head-ornaments (Drona, Karna Parvas). (Note: Two-horse chariots appear rarely for minor riders; Arjuna’s car is always drawn by four white steeds [Karna, Shalya Parvas]).

Positions of Charioteer & Warrior: The charioteer (sarathi) stands or sits forward on the front platform/pole-base holding reins and whip; the warrior stands upright behind/above him inside the fenced box (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). [LATER TRADITION]: Modern depictions often place the warrior in front of the driver or show open, rail-less low carts.

2. Warrior Banners (Dhvaja)

(Primary sources: Bhishma Parva Section 17; Drona Parva Sections 23 & 105; Karna Parva)

Arjuna: A fierce, gigantic celestial Ape (Vanara — bestowed by Agni/Vishvakarman, roaring to terrify enemies) with a long tail, surrounded by smaller banner-creatures/streamers (Adi, Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). [LATER TRADITION]: While later Puranic tradition explicitly identifies this ape as Hanuman seated on the flag, Vyasa’s Bhishma and Drona Parvas describe it strictly as a terrifying, roaring celestial ape (kapidhvaja) without naming Hanuman on the mast during battle.

Bhima: A gigantic silver Lion with eyes made of lapis lazuli (Drona Parva, Sec. 23).

Yudhishthira: A golden Moon surrounded by planets/stars (Drona Parva, Sec. 23).

Nakula: A golden-backed Sarabha (mythical eight-legged beast stronger than a lion) (Drona Parva, Sec. 23).

Sahadeva: A silver Swan (Hamsa) adorned with bells (Drona Parva, Sec. 23).   
Karna: A golden Elephant’s rope/girth (Nagakakshya) decked with garlands and bells (Bhishma Parva, Sec. 17; Drona Parva, Sec. 105; Karna Parva). [LATER TRADITION]: Popular modern art often gives Karna a sun emblem.   
Bhishma: A towering golden Palmyra palm tree (Tala) decked with five stars (Bhishma Parva, Sec. 17; Drona Parva, Sec. 105).   
Drona: A golden sacrificial Altar (Vedi) topped with a water-pot (Kamandalu) and a bow (Drona Parva, Sec. 105), or a black deer-skin (krishnajina) and water-pot (Drona Parva, Sec. 23).   
Ashvatthama: A golden Lion’s tail (Simhalangula) shining like the rising sun (Bhishma Parva, Sec. 17; Drona Parva, Sec. 105).   
Duryodhana: A jeweled golden Elephant (Naga) hung with a hundred tinkling bells (Bhishma Parva, Sec. 17; Drona Parva, Sec. 105). [LATER TRADITION]: Modern TV/art frequently assigns Duryodhana a hooded cobra/serpent banner.

Shalya: A golden image of the Goddess of Corn/Earth (Sita / Ploughshare) (Drona Parva, Sec. 105).

Kripa: A bovine Bull (Vrisha) (Drona Parva, Sec. 105).   
Jayadratha: A silver Boar (Varaha) with golden chains (Drona Parva, Sec. 105).

3. Weapons & Divine Astras

Gandiva (Arjuna’s Bow): Created by Brahma; smooth, knotless, of immense length, variegated with celestial colors and gold bosses, strung with an indestructible cord (Adi, Virata, Karna Parvas). Paired with two inexhaustible quivers (Adi, Virata Parvas).

Vijaya (Karna’s Bow): Forged by Vishvakarman for Indra (given via Parashurama); celestial, studded with golden stars/gems, producing a thunderous twang (Karna Parva).

Arrows (Shara / Naracha): Long, reed-shafted arrows fletched with heron, peacock, or vulture feathers and bound with gold wire (Drona, Karna Parvas); all-iron shafts (naracha) used to pierce heavy plate/elephant hide (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). Specialized tips include crescent-headed (ardhachandra, for cutting bows/heads), razor-headed (kshurapra), and calf-toothed (vatsadanta) (Drona, Karna Parvas).

Maces (Gada): Heavy, tapering, iron-bound clubs plated with gold bands, spikes, and studded gems, wielded two-handed by Bhima, Duryodhana, and Shalya (Drona, Karna, Shalya Parvas).

Spears & Darts (Shakti / Tomara): Heavy metal-shafted throwing darts/javelins. Karna’s Vasavi Shakti (given by Indra) is blazing, adorned with bells, plumed, and strikes like a meteor/lightning bolt once before returning to Indra (Vana, Udyoga, Drona Parvas). Yudhishthira’s golden dart (Shakti) kills Shalya (Shalya Parva).

Discus (Chakra): Sudarshana (Krishna’s weapon, though he does not wield it in battle after the vow, save briefly threatening Bhishma in Bhishma Parva)—razor-rimmed, fiery, spinning wheel of light. [LATER TRADITION]: Krishna is often shown casually spinning Sudarshana on his finger during the Gita; in Bhishma Parva, he leaps down holding it angrily to charge Bhishma.

Appearance of Loosed Divine Astras: Invoked by mantra onto an ordinary arrow, an astra transforms mid-flight into physical elemental/celestial phenomena (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas):

Agneyastra: Blazing sheets of fire and smoke (Drona Parva).

Varunastra: Torrential rain-clouds and floods extinguishing fire (Drona Parva).

Vayavyastra: Whirlwinds scattering clouds and armies (Drona Parva).

Narayanastra: Thousands of blazing arrows, fiery wheels, iron balls, and maces raining from the sky (Drona Parva).

Brahmastra / Brahmashiras: Blinding solar radiance, earthquakes, and meteors (Drona, Sauptika Parvas).

Nagastra (Karna’s Ashvasena arrow): A single blazing arrow that becomes a winged, venomous serpent breathing fire (Karna Parva).

4. Armour, Crowns, Dress & Heroic Appearance

Armour (Varman / Kavacha): Flexible coats of mail and segmented plate made of gold, burnished iron, or steel, embossed with suns, moons, and lotus motifs (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). Finger-guards (angulitra / godha) of iguana-skin bound with gold protect archers’ hands (Virata, Drona Parvas).

Headgear: Kings wear tall golden diadems/crowns (kirita or mukuta) studded with gems, or turbans (ushnisha) wound with gold (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). Arjuna wears Indra’s radiant Kirita diadem (Virata, Drona, Karna Parvas).

Dress & Ornaments: Lower garments (paridhana) of fine silk (yellow, white, or crimson) tucked for battle, sacred threads (yajnopavita), golden armlets (keyura), earrings (kundala), pearl/gold necklaces (nishka), and bodies smeared with red sandalwood paste (raktachandana) (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas).

Distinctive Heroic Appearances:

Bhishma: Aged yet towering and muscular, with snow-white hair, white turban, white garments, white armour, and white steeds, shining like a white mountain (Bhishma Parva).

Drona: Aged Brahmin general with white hair and beard, dressed in white robes over golden armour, carrying bow and arrows (Drona Parva).

Karna: Tall, broad-chested, radiant like Surya; without his innate golden kavacha-kundala during the Kurukshetra war (having given them to Indra in Vana Parva), wearing royal golden plate armour and fine earrings (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). [LATER TRADITION]: Modern art frequently depicts Karna still wearing his birth-armour at Kurukshetra.

Arjuna: Dark-complexioned (Shyama), youthful, long-armed, wearing the blazing Kirita crown, bow-string calluses on both arms, and red sandalwood paste (Virata, Drona, Karna Parvas).

Bhima & Duryodhana: Massive, broad-shouldered, bull-necked warriors; Duryodhana wears a tall golden crown and gem-studded golden mail (Karna, Shalya Parvas).

Krishna (as Charioteer): Dark like a blue cloud, wearing yellow silk (pitambara), Kaustubha gem on chest, golden armlets, peacock-feather crest or floral garland, holding reins and whip (unarmed) (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas).

5. War Elephants & Howdahs

Elephant Armour & Trappings: War elephants (Gaja / Matanga) are clad in heavy iron or leather mail-coats (varman), draped with colorful woolen blankets/rugs (kuthah), golden nets, pearl garlands, frontal plates on the forehead, and rows of large bronze/iron bells hung from their necks and flanks (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). Tusks are capped with sharp iron or golden spikes/ferrules (Drona Parva).

Howdahs / Seats: Vyasa rarely uses a late-medieval walled wooden "box howdah"; instead, the text describes armored neck-seats and back-platforms/housings (asana / kakshya) strapped tightly with thick girth-ropes (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas). [LATER TRADITION]: Ornate Mughal/Rajput-style domed wooden canopied howdahs are post-epic; in Vyasa’s text, kings (such as Bhagadatta on Supratika in Drona Parva) sit astride the elephant’s neck or within a low, iron-plated strapped housing/platform (varna) on its back.

Crew per Elephant: Typically carries seven warriors/attendants (Drona, Karna Parvas): one mahout (ankushadhara) on the neck wielding an iron goad (ankusha) and lance, one principal archer/king seated in the armored housing, two warriors guarding the flanks with javelins/swords, and foot-soldiers (padaraksha) guarding the elephant’s legs below (Bhishma, Drona, Karna Parvas).
