THE HOUSE OF KURU

One family.
Seven generations to the war and after.

  1. I

    Śantanu

    King of Hastināpura

    wed

    Gaṅgā

    The river goddess

    and later

    Satyavatī

    The fisherwoman; earlier, mother of Vyāsa

  2. II

    Bhīṣma

    Son of Gaṅgā. Took the terrible vow. Falls on day ten.

    Citrāṅgada & Vicitravīrya

    Sons of Satyavatī. Both die young and childless.

    Vyāsa

    Satyavatī's elder son, the sage. Fathers the next line; tells the whole story.

  3. III

    Dhṛtarāṣṭra

    Born blind. Wed to Gāndhārī, who binds her own eyes.

    Pāṇḍu

    The pale king. Wed to Kuntī and Mādrī. Dies in the forest.

    Vidura

    Son of a palace maid. The wise voice no one heeds.

    Śakuni

    Gāndhārī's brother, prince of Gāndhāra. Throws the dice.

  4. IV

    THE KAURAVAS · sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra

    Duryodhana

    The eldest of a hundred. Falls by the mace, on the last day.

    Duḥśāsana

    Drags Draupadī into the hall. Bhīma keeps his vow.

    Vikarṇa

    The one Kaurava who spoke for Draupadī.

    Duḥśalā

    The only daughter. Wed to Jayadratha.

    THE SECRET FIRSTBORN

    Karṇa

    Kuntī's son by the Sun, before her marriage. Raised by a charioteer. The eldest Pāṇḍava, who never knew it in time.

    THE PĀṆḌAVAS · sons of Pāṇḍu

    Yudhiṣṭhira

    Son of Kuntī by Dharma. The king who could not lie.

    Bhīma

    Son of Kuntī by Vāyu, the wind.

    Arjuna

    Son of Kuntī by Indra. Krishna's friend; hears the Gītā.

    Nakula & Sahadeva

    Twin sons of Mādrī by the Aśvins.

    Draupadī

    Born from the altar fire. Wife of all five.

  5. V

    Abhimanyu

    Arjuna's son by Subhadrā, Krishna's sister. Dies at sixteen in the wheel formation.

    Ghaṭotkaca

    Bhīma's son by Hiḍimbā. Falls to Karṇa's spear.

    The five Upapāṇḍavas

    Draupadī's sons, one by each brother. Killed in their sleep.

  6. VI

    Parīkṣit

    Abhimanyu's son by Uttarā, saved in the womb by Krishna. The last of the line, and the next king.

  7. VII

    Janamejaya

    Parīkṣit's son. At his great sacrifice, Vyāsa's pupil Vaiśampāyana tells him this whole story.

MAHĀBHĀRATA

The great epic, carved into a living wall.

A dramatised retelling of the Mahābhārata, carved as a living wall. Some details follow later tradition rather than the critical edition, and quoted words are retellings, not translations of the Sanskrit text.
